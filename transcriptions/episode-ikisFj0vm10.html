AI IN HOLLYWOOD: Interview with Lisa Oratz - Copyright Lawyer

Published: October 15, 2026
Lisa Oratz is senior counsel at Ashurst Perkins Coie, a technology, IP and entertainment lawyer who has advised filmmakers, studios, creators and big tech on AI. She tells Sophie where US copyright law stands on AI in 2026: there is no new statute, the Copyright Office's views are influential but are not law, and the one point she calls well established is that a work with no human authorship at all gets no copyright. She explains how AI-assisted films still get registered (select, arrange and modify the output, or start from your own drawings and storyboards), using the registration she says her team obtained for the DALL-E short Critterz. On infringement, she goes through the Bartz v. Anthropic and Kadrey v. Meta fair use rulings, why she says the practical risk for people using AI tools sits in the output and how they prompt, what studio licensing deals and tool indemnities cover, and how the UK and other countries treat the same questions. Lisa's views are her own, not those of her firm or its clients. Nothing in this episode is legal advice.
